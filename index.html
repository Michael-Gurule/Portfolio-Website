Current Work

The network already reported its health every five minutes. The hard part was reading it: hand-set thresholds that couldn't tell a daily traffic peak from a fault, and alerts that could only be grouped by whichever team a device belonged to.

  • ~1B series scored today
  • 6–12B series in scope
  • 8 network domains

It started with a single streaming app. Video is nearly fully onboarded, and the remaining domains are coming on one at a time.

Baselines that know the calendar

Telemetry swings by hour, day, month, and season, and fixed thresholds treated every peak as a fault. Each signal is now scored against a baseline that carries those cycles, so normal swings stop opening tickets.

Root cause, not team boundaries

Alerts used to be grouped only by the team a device was assigned to. Correlation now follows the network topology, tracing a degradation upstream to its source and out to the services, markets, device models, and software versions it touches.

Tuned by the pipeline, checked by people

A statistical scorer sets the floor, and an automated tuning pipeline retunes the model stack on its own. Drift alerts say when to look, and every flagged anomaly is reviewed and labeled. Those labels feed back into training.

  1. LiveDetection
  2. LiveCorrelation
  3. Near-term readyForecasting
  4. In designAgentic operations

Now defining the operating flow and guardrails for agents that act on what the platform finds, alongside security and policy review.
